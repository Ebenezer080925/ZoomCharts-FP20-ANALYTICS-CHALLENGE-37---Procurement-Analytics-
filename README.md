### 1.1 Gross Capital Expenditures & Seasonal Trends
- Spend-to-Budget Alignment: Total procurement spending over the multi-year cycle closed at 979.40M against a total benchmark budget allocation of 1.01bn. This indicates tight institutional
   cost management at the macro level.

- Extreme Seasonality Fluctuation: The Actual Spend VS Budget Monthly Trend line visual highlights a distinct cyclical wave. Spending exhibits massive peaks twice a year: a primary surge during mid-year (June–July)
   and a secondary peak at the beginning of the fiscal year (January). Conversely, procurement actions sharply hit their lowest points during late spring (April) and late winter (February).

- The Static Budget Baseline: Total budgeted allocations (the light-blue line) track almost completely flat across the year around the 81.62M monthly baseline, revealing that budgeted expectations are static
   while actual operational spending is highly reactive and seasonal.

### 1.2 Cost Savings & Variance Velocity
- Accelerating Savings Returns: Cumulative savings reached 104.27M. More importantly, the Prior Year (PY) comparison metrics show a massive 55.5% expansion (+37.2M) in savings velocity, proving that vendor
   negotiations or pricing contracts have grown drastically more efficient over the timeline.

- Positive Budget Variance: The company achieved a 33.66M (8.25%) budget variance surplus, showing they successfully operated under administrative cost benchmarks. This performance marks a 52.6% (+11.6M)
  improvement over the previous period's variance data.

### 1.3 Departmental & Categorical Spend Concentrators
- The Marketing & Operations Heavy-Weights: Requisition volume is heavily imbalanced. The Actual Spend VS Total Budget by Department bar visual identifies Marketing and Operations as the massive primary
  spend drivers, scaling significantly above the other eight internal business units.

- IT Software Monopoly: Out of the ten core categories, IT Software dominates the budget at 323.80M (nearly a third of global spend). Professional Services (176.18M) and Marketing (147.94M) rank as the
   second and third largest expenditure lines, while HR & Training (96.26M) anchors the lower end of the high-spend tier.

   across the business.
  
### 2.2 Maverick Spend Concentration by Human Requestors

individually. He is closely followed by Isla White, David Lee, and Nathan Young. The sharp downward step-trend in the bar visual confirms that policy bypassing is heavily concentrated among these top 4–5
individuals rather than being an equal problem across all corporate employees.

### 2.3 Single-Source Vendor Exposure

## SECTION 3: OPERATIONAL BOTTLENECK & SUPPLY CHAIN RISK
![](https://github.com/Ebenezer080925/ZoomCharts-FP20-ANALYTICS-CHALLENGE-37---Procurement-Analytics-/blob/main/Screenshot%202026-05-18%20154916.png)

### 3.1 AI Root-Cause Analysis on Logistical Turning Cycles
The global baseline delay across all transactions averages 4.08 days late. However, interactive decomposition analysis maps critical operational bottlenecks to specific intersections of risk, category,
and geography:
- Observation Path A (High-Risk Vendor Fragility): * Filtering for transactions managed by High-Risk Suppliers elevates the baseline delay to 4.15 days.

      -Drilling into categories within this risk layer exposes a major bottleneck in Facilities procurement, which suffers from a severe friction spike averaging 7.42 days late (nearly double the company average).

      -The system tracks 100% of this specific Facilities delay back to International orders originating from the Americas geographic region.

- Observation Path B (The Low-Risk Vendor Anomaly):

      -Conversely, checking Low-Risk Suppliers displays a surprising structural failure: a baseline delay of 5.44 days, which actively performs worse than the company average.

      -Root-cause breakdown isolates this anomaly directly to HR & Training categories.

      -When low-risk HR & Training services are sourced via International logistics lanes from Europe, delivery delays maximize at 6.02 days late, compared to their Local country counterpoints
      which close out much faster at 4.35 days late.
### 3.2 On-Time Delivery (OTD) Performance Constraints
- Global OTD Baseline: The overarching OTD percentage is locked at 64.13%, indicating that nearly 36% of all procurement transactions suffer an operational delay.

- Categorical Performance Gaps: The multi-dimensional risk matrix flags specific operational failure zones:

      -Facilities displays the lowest overall operational reliability, maintaining a weak 66.22% cumulative OTD rate.

      -IT Software represents a massive risk point due to its sheer scale—while it commands the most spend (323.80M), it maintains an unstable 67.70% OTD, heavily threatening the deployment
       timelines of internal IT infrastructure.
### 3.3 Accounts Payable Processing Backlogs
The funnel charts monitoring transactional progression reveal massive administrative blockages in the financial closing loops:

- Invoice Lifecycle Stalls: Out of the global PO pool, 2,090 transactions sit frozen in a Pending state, while 810 transactions are actively Overdue and 650 are trapped in Disputed status.

- Payment Settlement Disconnects: A large transactional volume is actively bottlenecked during settlement, with 1,760 invoices Pending, 990 placed On Hold, and 750 completely Overdue. This wide gap between
  the Count of PO Number by Invoice Status and the Count of PO Number by Payment Status proves that the company has a severe administrative lag in processing vendor payments after goods have been physically delivered.
